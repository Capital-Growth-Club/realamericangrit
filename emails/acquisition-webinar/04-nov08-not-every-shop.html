Buying a shop is great when you do it right. When you don’t, it costs you.

From Tom Howard

Not every shop I bought was a winner.

{{contact.first_name}}, I’ve bought more than 40 shops.

And I’d love to tell you every one of them was a home run.

They weren’t.

Buying a shop is one of the best moves you can make in this business — when you do it right.

But there’s a version of this that goes the other way.

You buy a shop that looked great on paper. Then you get inside it.

The numbers don’t hold up. The customers don’t stick the way you thought they would. The problems you inherited cost more than the revenue you bought.

Now you’re upside down in a deal that isn’t making you money.

Actually, it’s worse than that.

It’s making you less money than you were making before you bought it.

That’s the last place I want you to end up.

The good news? The signs are almost always there before you sign — if you know what you’re looking for.

I learned most of them the hard way. On December 2nd, I’ll walk you through what I look at now before I buy anything.
